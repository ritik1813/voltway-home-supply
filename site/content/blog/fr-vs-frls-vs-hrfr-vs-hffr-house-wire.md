Most people choosing house wire spend their time on the brand and almost none on the letters printed after it. That is the wrong way round. A genuine 2.5 sq mm coil from Polycab, Havells or Finolex carries the same current; what changes between FR, FRLS, HRFR and HFFR is how the insulation behaves when something goes wrong. Here is what each grade means, what it costs relative to the others, and which one we suggest for a flat in Gurgaon.

## The four grades in one table

| | FR | FRLS | HRFR | HFFR / LSZH |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full name | Flame retardant | Flame retardant, low smoke | Heat resistant, flame retardant | Halogen-free flame retardant (low smoke, zero halogen) |
| Insulation | PVC | PVC with low-smoke additives | Heat-resistant PVC | Halogen-free compound, no PVC |
| Resists catching fire | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Smoke when it burns | Dense, black | Much less | Dense, like FR | Very little, light in colour |
| Acid gas when it burns | High | Reduced | High | Practically none |
| Working temperature of insulation | 70 °C | 70 °C | 85 °C | 70 to 90 °C, depending on the product |
| Price (indicative) | Lowest | A step above FR | A step above FR | Highest |
| Typical use | Independent houses, low-rise | High-rise flats, offices | Circuits that run warm, hot locations | Hospitals, premium homes, escape routes |

Prices move with copper and are quoted per coil at the time of order, so we do not print numbers. As a rule the difference between grades inside one brand is larger than the difference between brands in the same grade.

## What the letters actually mean

**FR (flame retardant).** The PVC is compounded so that it resists ignition and stops burning when the flame is removed. This is the minimum you should accept. Ordinary non-FR wire still exists in the market at tempting prices and has no place inside a wall.

**FRLS (flame retardant, low smoke).** Everything FR does, plus additives that cut the smoke and the hydrochloric acid gas the insulation gives off when it burns. In a building fire it is smoke, not flame, that blocks the staircase and causes most injuries, which is why FRLS is the sensible grade for any multi-storey building. On coils and in the Indian standard you will also see it written as FR-LSH.

**HRFR (heat resistant, flame retardant).** Ordinary PVC insulation is rated for a conductor temperature of 70 °C. HRFR insulation is rated for 85 °C, so it tolerates heat better before it softens or ages. It is flame retardant like FR, but it is not a low-smoke grade unless the coil also says so.

**HFFR, also sold as LSZH or ZHFR (halogen-free, low smoke zero halogen).** The insulation contains no chlorine at all, so when it burns there is very little smoke and no corrosive gas. It is the cleanest-burning option and the most expensive. It is also a little stiffer to pull through conduit, which electricians notice.

## How the grades are tested

The claims are not marketing. PVC house wire is made to IS 694, and the flame-retardant grades have to pass defined tests:

- **Oxygen index:** how much oxygen the insulation needs to keep burning. Air is about 21 percent oxygen; FR grades need a minimum of 29 percent, so they do not sustain a flame in normal air.
- **Temperature index:** the temperature at which the insulation would burn in normal air, minimum 250 °C.
- **Smoke density:** how much light passes through the smoke in a test chamber. Low-smoke grades must let at least 40 percent through.
- **Acid gas emission:** the hydrochloric acid gas released on burning, limited to 20 percent by weight for low-smoke grades and close to zero for halogen-free wire.

Halogen-free wire has its own standard, IS 17048. If a coil claims a grade, the grade and the ISI mark should be printed along the wire itself, not only on the box.

## Which is better, FRLS or HRFR?

This is the question we are asked most, and the two are not really competing. FRLS answers "what happens in a fire". HRFR answers "how well does the insulation cope with heat in daily use".

- In a **high-rise flat**, smoke is the bigger risk. Choose FRLS.
- On **circuits that run warm**, such as air conditioners, geysers, the kitchen and any conduit packed with many wires, HRFR gives the insulation more margin. So does a top-floor flat under a terrace in a Gurgaon summer.
- If you want both properties, look for a wire printed HR FR-LSH, which is heat resistant and low smoke together. Polycab Green Wire is one example.

One caution: a higher temperature rating is not a reason to use a thinner wire. Size the wire by the load and the standard tables, then choose the grade.

## What the brands call them

Brand names for grades confuse buyers more than anything else, because the name on the box is a range name, not a grade.

- **Polycab:** Green Wire is a heat-resistant, low-smoke, lead-free wire (printed HR FR-LSH LF). It is not a halogen-free wire, although many buyers assume so from the name. Polycab's halogen-free wire is labelled HFFR.
- **Havells:** Life Line Plus is the heat-resistant HRFR wire, Life Guard is the low-smoke FR-LSH wire and Life Shield is the halogen-free HFFR wire.
- **Finolex:** labels its house wire by grade, FR and FR-LSH.

Range names change, so read the grade printed on the wire rather than trusting the box design. We compare the three brands themselves in [Polycab vs Havells vs Finolex](/blog/polycab-vs-havells-vs-finolex-house-wire/).

## Which grade for which home

- **High-rise flat, new wiring or full rewiring:** FRLS throughout. If the budget allows, HFFR for the whole flat or at least for the passage and bedrooms.
- **Builder floor or independent house:** FR is acceptable, FRLS is better and the extra cost on a full house is modest.
- **AC, geyser and kitchen circuits:** FRLS in the correct size is fine. HRFR or a heat-resistant low-smoke wire is a reasonable upgrade on long or heavily loaded runs.
- **Hospitals, clinics, schools, server rooms, basements and escape routes:** HFFR, which is usually what the consultant specifies anyway.
- **Projects:** follow the specification. Most Gurgaon residential specifications now ask for FRLS as the base grade.

Whatever you choose, keep one brand and one grade across all sizes in the flat, so the colours, insulation and pulling behaviour stay consistent.

## Mistakes we see on wire lists

1. **"Polycab 2.5 sq mm" with no grade.** The quote then comes back in the cheapest grade. Always write the grade.
2. **FRLS for the rooms and loose leftover wire for the AC.** The heaviest circuits deserve the best wire, not the oldest coil on site.
3. **Paying for a better grade and receiving FR.** Check the printing on the wire against the invoice on delivery.
4. **Assuming a better grade allows a smaller size.** It does not.
5. **Buying on price from an unknown counter.** A fake coil in any grade is worse than a genuine FR coil. The checks are in our wire brand comparison.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which is better, FR or FRLS wire?

FRLS. Both resist catching fire, but FRLS also produces much less smoke and acid gas if it does burn. In a multi-storey building that difference matters more than anything else about the wire. FR remains acceptable for a low-rise independent house.

### Is HRFR wire better than FRLS?

They do different jobs. HRFR insulation is rated for a higher temperature, 85 °C against 70 °C, so it copes better with heat in daily use. FRLS gives off less smoke in a fire. For a high-rise flat FRLS is the priority; HRFR is useful on AC, geyser and kitchen circuits.

### Is HFFR or LSZH wire worth the extra cost for a home?

It is the safest grade in a fire because it gives off very little smoke and no corrosive gas. It costs the most and is slightly stiffer to install. It is worth considering for a premium flat, for bedrooms and passages, and for anyone who wants the best available; FRLS is a sound choice where the budget is tighter.

### How do I know which grade of wire I have been given?

The grade is printed along the insulation at regular intervals together with the brand, size and ISI mark. Read it on the wire itself, compare it with your GST invoice, and reject coils where the printing is missing or does not match what you ordered.

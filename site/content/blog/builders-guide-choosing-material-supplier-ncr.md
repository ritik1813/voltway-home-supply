On a tower project the electrical and plumbing material bill is large, spread over a year or more, and delivered in dozens of lots to a site where idle labour costs more per day than any discount. Choosing the supplier is therefore less about the lowest rate on the first BOQ and more about whether the material will be genuine, on time, documented and priced predictably through the last phase. Here is what to check.

## 1. Authorised sourcing and documentation

Your consultant will ask for it, your client's auditor may ask for it, and your insurer will certainly ask for it after any incident. The supplier should be able to:

- name the brands on the GST invoice, line by line;
- provide manufacturer test certificates for wire and cable lots on request;
- provide warranty cards or registration for switchgear, fans and fittings;
- confirm the distribution channel for each major brand.

A supplier who quotes Polycab or Havells well below market and will not put the brand on the invoice is not a supplier you want on a tower.

## 2. Rate holding and validity

Copper moves weekly; sanitaryware moves annually. Agree how rates behave:

- **Quote validity** for each category (7 days for wire and cable is typical, 30 or more for fittings and sanitaryware).
- **Rate holding on confirmed phases**: once a phase is confirmed, the rate for that phase does not move.
- **Price-variation clause** for long projects on wire and cable, tied to a published copper index, so neither side is gambling.

Ambiguity here is the usual source of mid-project disputes.

## 3. Dispatch capacity and scheduling

Ask how the supplier handles a weekly draw across two or three towers. You want:

- a named coordinator for the site;
- a fixed delivery day or a 24- to 48-hour response on stocked items anywhere in Gurgaon and NCR;
- phase-wise dispatch matched to the programme (conduit at slab, wire at pulling, DBs and switches at finishing; pipes and concealed bodies early, CP and sanitaryware late);
- packing and labelling per tower or per floor where the site asks for it;
- a clear process for shortages and top-ups at the held rate.

A trial phase (see below) shows this better than any promise.

## 4. Substitution policy

Every long project hits a shortage: a switch series discontinued, a CPVC size short, a fan model superseded. The policy you want is **no silent substitution**: the supplier flags the shortage on the quote or before dispatch, proposes an equivalent from the approved-makes list, and waits for approval. Write it into the order.

## 5. Running account and billing

For multi-tower work, a single account with:

- consolidated GST invoices per dispatch or per period, as your accounts team prefers;
- a ledger you can reconcile against the BOQ by tower and by phase;
- agreed payment terms that step up after the first phases;
- clear handling of returns and damaged goods.

Avoid a supplier who treats each dispatch as a fresh cash sale; the reconciliation cost on your side will exceed any saving.

## 6. Category depth

A supplier who is strong in wire and resells plumbing at a markup, or vice versa, costs you on the weaker category and gives you two sets of problems under one name. Ask which brands they stock directly in each of electrical, plumbing and sanitary, and home automation if the project includes a smart-home package per unit.

## 7. Location and reach

For a Gurgaon site a supplier within the district can respond same day. For NCR sites in Noida, Faridabad or Ghaziabad the question is whether the supplier has a dispatch rhythm for that destination (a fixed weekly run, transport arrangements) rather than treating it as a one-off.

## Running a trial phase

Before committing the project, place one phase, for example the conduit and boxes for one tower's slabs or the concealed plumbing for one tower, with the supplier you are evaluating. Measure:

1. Did the quote match the BOQ line by line, with substitutions marked?
2. Did material land on the agreed day, correctly packed?
3. Was documentation (invoice with brands, test certificates) delivered without chasing?
4. How were the inevitable top-ups handled, and at what rate?
5. Was there one person to call, and did they answer?

A supplier who passes a trial phase on these five points will usually serve the whole project; one who does not will not improve as the stakes rise.

## Red flags

- Rates well below market on branded wire or switchgear.
- Reluctance to name brands on the invoice.
- "We will adjust the rate at dispatch."
- No named site coordinator.
- Pressure to take the whole project's material in one lot "to lock the rate".

## Frequently asked questions

### Should a builder buy material directly or let the contractor buy?

Buying directly gives the developer control over brand, grade and rate and removes the contractor's markup, at the cost of managing supply. Many Gurgaon projects split it: the developer buys major items (wire, switchgear, pipes, sanitaryware) on a running account and the contractor buys consumables. Either way, the supplier checks above apply.

### How do we handle copper price changes on a year-long project?

Agree a price-variation clause for wire and cable tied to a published copper or metal index, with rates held per confirmed phase. This protects both sides and avoids renegotiating every month.

### Can one supplier serve towers in Gurgaon and Noida on the same project?

Yes, if they have a dispatch rhythm for both. Ask for the delivery schedule for each site and a single account with per-site invoicing so reconciliation stays simple.

### What documentation should the supplier provide for the consultant?

GST invoices naming brand and grade per line, manufacturer test certificates for wire and cable lots, and warranty documents for switchgear, fans and fittings. Agree this list before the first dispatch.

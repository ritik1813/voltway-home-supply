Wire and MCBs are hidden once the job is done. Switches are the part of the electrical work you look at and touch every day for the next fifteen years, and they are also where the price range is widest: the same flat can be fitted for a modest sum or for several times that, with no difference in safety. Here is how the four names we are asked for most compare, and how to avoid the common ways of wasting money on them.

Range names are updated every few years and each brand's tiers overlap, so treat this as a rough guide to where the ranges sit rather than a permanent list. Rates are quoted against your module list from the current price list at the time of order.

## Which is cheaper?

Tier matters far more than brand. Roughly, from lowest to highest:

1. **Entry:** Anchor Penta and Ziva, GM G-Magic, Legrand Britzy and Allzy, Schneider Livia.
2. **Mainstream:** Anchor Roma, Legrand Mylinc and Lyncus, Schneider Opale, GM Four Five.
3. **Upper and premium:** Legrand Myrius and Arteor, Schneider ZENcelo, Unica and AvatarOn, Anchor Vision, GM Casa Viva.

Within one tier the brands sit close to each other on a full flat. Between the entry tier and the premium tier the bill can multiply, and most of that jump is in the plates, not the switches. Glass, metal and coloured plates are where premium ranges earn their price.

## What actually differs

**Safety is not the difference.** All four are ISI-marked, with switches made to IS 3854 and sockets to IS 1293. A genuine entry-range switch is as safe as a premium one.

**Feel.** The rocker action, the click and how flush the switch sits in the plate. This is personal; press the samples at the counter before deciding.

**Plates and colours.** Entry ranges come in white and perhaps one or two colours. Premium ranges offer grey, black, metallic, glass and wood finishes. White plates from any decent brand stay white; unbranded lookalikes are the ones that yellow.

**The module catalogue.** A range is only as useful as the modules available in it. Before choosing, check that the same range has everything you need:

- 6A or 10A switches for lights and fans, and two-way switches for bedrooms and passages;
- 16A switches and shuttered 16A sockets for power points;
- 20A, 25A or 32A double-pole switches for geysers and air conditioners;
- fan regulators, preferably the step type, which do not hum;
- USB charging sockets, TV and data sockets, bell pushes;
- dimmers, motion sensors or smart modules if you plan to use them.

Legrand and Schneider have the deepest catalogues at the premium end. Anchor Roma has the best availability of everyday modules: almost any electrical counter in NCR can replace a Roma switch the same day.

## Mistakes that cost money

1. **Choosing the range after the boxes are fixed.** Concealed metal boxes go into the wall before plaster and are sized by module count: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 and so on. Some premium ranges need their own boxes or support frames. Decide the range before the electrician chases the walls.
2. **Mixing ranges.** A plate from one range does not accept modules from another, even within the same brand. One range for the whole flat keeps replacement simple.
3. **A 6A switch on a geyser or AC.** Heavy loads need a 20A or higher double-pole switch that cuts both live and neutral. This is the most common safety mistake we see on switch lists.
4. **No spares.** Buy about five percent extra modules and two or three spare plates. Ranges get discontinued, and one cracked plate in a discontinued colour can mean changing a whole room.
5. **Counting plates instead of modules.** An electrician's list that says "30 switch boards" cannot be priced. Ask for a list by module: how many 6A switches, 16A sockets, regulators and blanks, and which plate sizes.
6. **Buying loose pieces on price.** High-volume ranges are the ones counterfeiters copy. Buy sealed boxes from an authorised channel with a GST invoice.

## Which to choose

- **Rental flat or tight budget:** Anchor Penta or Ziva, or GM G-Magic. Safe, inexpensive and easy to replace.
- **Your own flat, sensible budget:** Anchor Roma, Legrand Lyncus or Mylinc, or Schneider Opale. This tier is where most Gurgaon flats land, and all three will serve well.
- **Premium interior:** Legrand Myrius or Arteor, or Schneider ZENcelo or Unica. Choose on the plate finish your designer wants, then confirm every module is available in it.
- **Project or multi-tower:** the consultant's approved-makes list usually names two or three of Legrand, Schneider, Anchor and Havells. Pick the one your supplier can deliver consistently across phases, because top-ups in a mixed lot cause snag-list problems.

If the distribution board is going to be Legrand or Schneider, taking switches from the same brand keeps one warranty and one service contact. It is a convenience, not a requirement. ## Frequently asked questions

### Which is better, Legrand or Schneider switches?

Neither is better in a way that affects safety or life. Both are ISI-marked, both are accepted by consultants and both have deep catalogues. Choose on the feel of the switch, the plate finish you like and which range your supplier can deliver in full, including spares.

### Is Anchor Roma a good choice for a flat?

Yes. Roma is the most widely available modular range in India, it is reasonably priced and replacements can be found in almost any market. The finish is plainer than premium ranges, which is the main reason people pay more for Legrand or Schneider.

### Which modular switches are cheapest?

The entry ranges: Anchor Penta and Ziva, GM G-Magic, Legrand Britzy and Allzy and Schneider Livia. They are safe and ISI-marked. The saving compared with a mainstream range is real but not large on a single flat; the big jump in cost comes at the premium tier.

### Can I mix switches and plates from different brands?

No. Plates and modules are made to fit their own range, and a module from another brand or even another range of the same brand will not seat properly. Choose one range for the whole flat and keep a few spare modules and plates.
